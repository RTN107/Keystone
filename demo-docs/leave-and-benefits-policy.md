# Leave and Benefits Policy

This policy sets out the leave and benefits for every permanent employee based in Bengaluru. Entitlements run on the Indian financial year, April to March, unless stated. Where it conflicts with the Karnataka Shops and Commercial Establishments Act, 1961, or any other applicable law, the law applies and People Operations will explain the difference. Contractors are covered by their own agreements, not by this policy. Questions about any section go to People Operations.

## Leave year and how to apply

The leave year follows the financial year, 1 April to 31 March. Balances are credited on the schedules below and shown on your payslip. Apply for leave through your line manager as early as you can, and see Requesting leave for notice. Leave is counted in whole or half days. Saturdays and Sundays are not counted as leave, and a public holiday inside a period of leave is not deducted from your balance.

## Earned leave (annual leave)

Every permanent employee earns 18 days of paid annual leave in each leave year. It is called earned leave, or privilege leave, and is given in line with the Karnataka Shops and Commercial Establishments Act, 1961. It accrues at 1.5 days a month from the date of joining. Up to 15 unused days of annual leave carry into the next year and must be used by 30 June, after which they lapse. A new joiner can take it only as it accrues, in whole or half days.

## Casual leave

Casual leave is for short-notice personal needs. Every permanent employee gets 8 days a year, credited in full on 1 April and pro-rated for joiners. It cannot be carried forward or encashed, and no more than 3 days can be taken at a stretch. Casual leave cannot be combined with earned leave to cover a planned absence of more than 5 working days; use earned leave for planned absence.

## Sick leave

Employees receive 12 days of paid sick leave a year, covering physical and mental health equally. Tell your line manager before your normal start time on the first day if you can. A medical certificate from a registered practitioner is needed after 2 consecutive days. If you fall ill during earned leave and have a certificate, the sick days are returned to your earned leave. Unused sick leave does not carry over or pay out.

## Requesting leave

Request leave through your line manager. Give at least two weeks' notice for any absence longer than five consecutive working days. Your manager responds within three working days. Leave that spans a major client date also needs your team lead's agreement. Approved leave goes in the shared calendar so teams can plan around it, and a refusal is always discussed with you in person. Leave of more than 15 working days at a stretch also needs People Operations to agree.

## Public holidays

The company observes 12 paid holidays a year from the Karnataka government list, with dates published in January: Makara Sankranti, Republic Day, Ugadi, Ramzan, Labour Day, Independence Day, Ganesh Chaturthi, Gandhi Jayanti, Mahanavami (Ayudha Puja), Kannada Rajyotsava on 1 November, Deepavali (Balipadyami) and Christmas. A holiday that falls on a Saturday or Sunday is not replaced with another day.

## Optional holidays

Each employee may take 2 optional holidays a year in addition to the 12, chosen from: Good Friday, Maha Shivaratri, Basava Jayanti, Bakrid, Muharram, Raksha Bandhan, Onam, Eid-e-Milad, Dr B R Ambedkar Jayanti and Kanakadasa Jayanti. Choose by 31 March for the year and tell your line manager at least a week ahead. Optional holidays do not carry forward or pay out. Staff who observe a festival that is not on the list can speak to People Operations.

## Working on a holiday

An employee who is asked to work on a public holiday or a weekly off gets a compensatory day off within 30 days, agreed with their line manager. The request to work must come from the line manager or delivery lead in writing beforehand, and the employee may decline a request that comes with less than two days' notice. Compensatory days do not carry past the leave year and are not paid out unless the employee leaves.

## Maternity benefit

Under the Maternity Benefit Act, 1961 (as amended in 2017) an eligible woman employee receives 26 weeks of paid maternity leave for her first two children and 12 weeks from the third, with up to 8 weeks taken before the expected delivery date. Eligibility is 80 days of work in the 12 months before the expected date. Pay is the average daily wage. Tell People Operations in writing at least eight weeks before the expected date so that cover can be planned.

## Other maternity-related leave

Miscarriage or medical termination of pregnancy: 6 weeks of paid leave on a medical certificate. Tubectomy: 2 weeks. Illness arising from pregnancy or delivery: up to 1 month more. An adopting mother, or a commissioning mother, of a child under three months gets 12 weeks. After maternity leave, a role that allows it can be done from home by agreement for a period. The company has no crèche, so it pays ₹6,000 a month per child until age 3 instead.

## Paternity leave

There is no private-sector statute for paternity leave, so this is company policy. The father or non-birthing parent receives 10 working days of paid leave, taken within 6 months of the birth or adoption, in one block or two. Adoption and surrogacy are treated like birth. Role and pay review are protected during the leave. Give four weeks' notice to your line manager and People Operations, or as soon as you can if the birth is early.

## Provident fund

Every permanent employee is enrolled in the Employees' Provident Fund. Both you and the company contribute 12% of basic wages. The company's share is split between the EPF account and the pension scheme, which is calculated on the statutory wage ceiling of ₹15,000 a month. Your Universal Account Number (UAN) stays with you across jobs, so give your existing UAN at joining and transfer the old balance. Contributions appear on your payslip each month.

## Gratuity

Under the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972, you are eligible for gratuity after five years of continuous service, or earlier on death or disablement. It is 15 days of last drawn basic salary for every completed year, divided by 26 working days, up to the statutory ceiling of ₹20 lakh. The company pays within 30 days of your last working day. Nominate a beneficiary in writing at joining and update it after marriage or the birth of a child.

## ESI and professional tax

The Employees' State Insurance scheme applies only to employees whose gross wages are ₹21,000 a month or less; contributions are 0.75% from the employee and 3.25% from the company. Most roles earn more, so most employees are covered by the group medical policy instead. Professional tax is deducted under the Karnataka slab: ₹200 a month, and ₹300 in February, for monthly salaries of ₹25,000 and above. It appears on your payslip.

## Payroll and income tax

Salary is paid on the last working day of each month by bank transfer. Income tax is deducted at source under section 192 according to the regime you choose in April, new or old, and you can change regime only at year end. Investment proofs under the old regime are collected in January. Form 16 is issued by 15 June. Reimbursements are paid with the next month's salary if they are claimed by the 20th.

## Notice period by level

Notice applies to resignation and, except for cause, to termination:
| Level | Notice |
|---|---|
| Analyst and associate | 30 days |
| Consultant | 60 days |
| Senior consultant and lead | 60 days |
| Manager and above | 90 days |
You may buy out unserved notice by paying salary for the shortfall, and the company may waive notice in writing. Earned leave cannot be used to shorten notice.

## Exit and final settlement

On exit the company encashes unused earned leave, up to 30 days, at basic salary divided by 26 for each day. Casual and sick leave lapse. Gratuity and provident fund are handled as set out above. The full and final settlement is paid within 45 days of the last working day, once the line manager confirms that work has been handed over. A relieving letter and an experience letter follow the settlement.

## Hybrid working

After the 6 month probation, employees work in a hybrid pattern: three days a week at the Bengaluru base, usually Tuesday to Thursday, and the rest from home. Days spent at a client's site count as base days. Teams that cover US or UK clients on overlap shifts may work those shifts from home with their line manager's approval. A line manager can ask a team to meet at the base on a specific day with a week's notice.

## Working from another city

With line manager approval, an employee may work from a place other than their Bengaluru home for up to 20 working days a year, for example to look after a family member. Longer periods need People Operations approval. Work from outside India is not permitted without prior written approval, because of tax and data protection rules. Normal hours of 9:30 to 18:30 IST apply wherever you work from.

## Group medical insurance

All permanent employees are covered from the first day of employment under the group medical policy: a family floater with a sum insured of ₹5,00,000, covering the employee, spouse and up to two children. The company pays the full premium. Cover includes hospital stays, day-care procedures, pre- and post-hospitalisation costs and an annual health check. Treatment is cashless at empanelled hospitals; elsewhere claims are reimbursed. Pre-existing conditions are covered from day one.

## Parents and dependants cover

Parents or parents-in-law can be added to the policy for a sum insured of ₹3,00,000 each; the company pays half the premium and the employee pays the rest through payroll. A spouse or a newborn child is added within 30 days of the marriage or birth, with cover starting from that date. Outside that window, additions wait for the annual renewal in April. Maternity cover under the policy is ₹75,000 for a normal delivery and ₹1,00,000 for a caesarean.

## Learning budget

Each employee has a learning budget of ₹30,000 per financial year for courses, books and certification exams. The budget does not carry over. Ask your line manager before you book, so the course fits your development plan. Submit the receipt or a GST invoice within 30 days of payment and the amount is reimbursed with your next salary. A course paid for in one year but finished in the next is claimed in the year it was paid.

## Certification exams

Exam fees for a certification are reimbursed once you have passed, from the learning budget, and you also get one paid study day for each exam. If a certification costs more than ₹60,000 in a year, for example a premium cloud or data programme, it needs the approval of your line manager and People Operations and a 12 month retention agreement: leave within 12 months and you repay the cost pro rata. A failed exam can be retaken once, with the fee paid from the budget.
