# Pricing and Proposal Playbook

This playbook sets out how we price, discount and propose AI services work for clients in India and abroad. It applies to every new engagement and every renewal. Read it before you quote anything. All amounts are in Indian rupees (INR) and exclude GST unless stated. Our pricing year follows the financial year, April to March. The Head of Sales owns this document and reviews it each April. Questions it does not answer go to the Head of Sales.

## How we price

We sell AI and automation work in three shapes: fixed scope, a monthly retainer, or time and materials. Fixed scope is the default for anything we can specify in writing. A retainer suits the ongoing running and improvement of agents and workflows. Time and materials is reserved for discovery work, where the scope cannot yet be written down. Pick the shape before you pick the price, and say in the proposal why it fits.

## Fixed scope pricing

A fixed scope price is built from the days each role will spend, at the rate card, plus a contingency of 15% for risks named in the proposal. It is billed by milestones, never as a single invoice at the end. The assumptions the price depends on are listed in the proposal, and anything outside them is priced separately as a change request at the rate card. Do not quote a fixed price for work whose scope is still being discovered.

## Retainers and the retainer minimum

A retainer is quoted as a number of days a month at the rate card. The minimum is 4 days a month, and no retainer is sold below ₹1,60,000 a month before GST. Days are used in the month they are bought; up to two unused days roll into the next month and no more. A retainer runs for a fixed term of 3, 6, 12 or 24 months and renews for the same term unless either side gives 60 days' written notice.

## Time and materials

Time and materials is for discovery and for work where the client cannot yet describe the outcome. We quote a not-to-exceed estimate, bill monthly in arrears on approved timesheets, and tell the client when 75% of the estimate is used. We do not exceed the estimate without the client's written agreement. If discovery produces a scope that can be specified, the next phase is priced as fixed scope.

## Rate card

Standard day rates in INR, per person per day, before GST:
| Role | Experience | Day rate |
|---|---|---|
| Solution architect | 12+ years | ₹65,000 |
| Senior consultant | 8 to 12 years | ₹45,000 |
| Consultant | 4 to 8 years | ₹30,000 |
| Analyst | up to 4 years | ₹18,000 |
A day is eight hours between 9:30 and 18:30 IST. Rates are reviewed each April and apply from 1 April.

## Rate card terms

Work outside 9:30 to 18:30 IST on a weekday, on a Saturday, or on a Karnataka public holiday is charged at 1.5 times the day rate. Half days are charged at 60% of the day rate. A quoted price holds for 30 days. A renewal that starts after 1 April moves to the new card, and the client is told at least 60 days before. A role that is not on the card is priced by the Head of Sales.

## International clients

Clients outside India are quoted from a separate card in US dollars, per person per day: architect $950, senior consultant $650, consultant $430, analyst $260. The card is set once a year in April from the INR card at a planning rate and is not recalculated mid-year. Contracts in pounds or euros use the dollar card converted at the rate on the date of the proposal, rounded up to the nearest 10. The contract currency is stated on the first page of the proposal.

## Currency risk and export billing

For international contracts of up to twelve months, the rate is fixed in the contract and we bear the currency risk. Longer contracts carry an annual review clause tied to the RBI reference rate for the contract currency. Invoices are raised in the contract currency. Exports of services are zero-rated for GST under a Letter of Undertaking, so no GST is charged. Payment is expected within 30 days to our foreign currency account, and the FIRC is kept with the invoice.

## Commitment discounts

Clients who commit to a twelve month retainer can be offered up to 10% off the monthly retainer fee. A six month commitment qualifies for up to 5%. A commitment shorter than six months earns no discount. Discounts apply to our labour only, never to third party licences or cloud costs that we pass through at cost. A discount is agreed in writing in the proposal, shown as its own line so the client can see the standard rate, and never applied to work already delivered.

## Worked example of a commitment discount

A twelve month retainer of 8 days a month with a senior consultant is 8 × ₹45,000 = ₹3,60,000 a month at the standard rate. With the 10% twelve month discount it is ₹3,24,000 a month, a discount of ₹36,000, shown as its own line. GST at 18% (₹58,320) is added on top, so the client is invoiced ₹3,82,320 a month. A client who leaves before twelve months repays the discount already received.

## Approving larger discounts

Any discount above 10% needs written approval from the Head of Sales before it is mentioned to the client. A discount above 20% also needs sign-off from the Delivery Director, because margin below 30% puts delivery quality at risk. We never offer a discount in a first meeting. Record every approval in the deal notes with the approver's name and the date. An approval covers one deal only and expires after 30 days if the proposal is not signed.

## Discount authority by role

Who may agree a discount within the commitment tiers, and what each person is protecting:
| Discount | Who agrees it | Protecting |
|---|---|---|
| up to 5% (six month tier) | account lead | consistent pricing across clients |
| over 5% to 10% (twelve month tier) | sales manager | the account's margin |
Anything larger has its own rule, set out in the section on larger discounts. Every agreement is written, per deal, and kept in the deal notes.

## Paid pilots

We offer a paid two-week pilot at a fixed ₹5,00,000 plus GST. If the client signs a project within 45 days of the pilot ending, the full pilot fee is credited against the first invoice. Pilots are not discounted, and they are not a way around a discount conversation. A pilot ends with a short findings report and a recommendation, delivered within three business days of the last pilot day. A pilot is not extended; a second pilot needs the Head of Sales.

## GST on proposals

GST at 18% applies to all our services in India. A proposal shows the price before GST, the GST as its own line, and the total. For a client in Karnataka it is split as CGST 9% and SGST 9%; for a client in any other state it is IGST 18%. The proposal states the client's GSTIN and the place of supply. If the client has no GSTIN, ask finance before quoting. Never quote a GST-inclusive figure on its own.

## TDS and receipts

Indian clients deduct tax at source under section 194J: 10% on fees for professional services and 2% on fees for technical services, on the amount before GST where GST is shown as a separate line. The proposal says which rate we expect. The client pays us the balance and gives us Form 16A for the tax deducted. Finance follows up a missing Form 16A at 30 days. Quote and invoice the gross amount, never the amount after TDS.

## Payment terms

Domestic invoices are payable within 30 days of the invoice date. Fixed scope work is billed by milestone: 30% on signing, 40% when UAT starts and 30% on go-live. Retainers are billed monthly in advance on the 1st. Time and materials is billed monthly in arrears on approved timesheets. Payment is by bank transfer (NEFT or RTGS). Invoices carry the PO number where the client issues one, and a missing PO is chased before the invoice is sent.

## Late payment

An invoice unpaid at its due date gets a reminder the next day and a call from the account lead on day 7. Interest at 1.5% a month applies from day 8, as the contract states. At 45 days overdue the Head of Sales and the finance controller decide whether to pause new work, with seven days' written notice to the client. Do not promise or withdraw interest in conversation: a waiver needs the finance controller's written approval.

## Proposal structure

Every proposal has the same sections in the same order, so clients can compare ours with others: scope, assumptions, timeline, team, price, risks and terms. The price section shows the engagement shape, the rate card basis, any discount as its own line, GST, and the payment schedule. Use the current proposal template. Proposals go out within three business days of the discovery call, and each names one person at the client who can accept it.

## Proposal validity and review

Every proposal is valid for 30 days from its date. Proposals above ₹40 lakh are read by a second account lead before they go out. Proposals above ₹1 crore are also read by the Head of Sales and the finance controller. A proposal dated before 31 March for work starting after 1 April quotes the new year's rate card. A reviewer's comments are recorded in the deal notes, with the date.

## Travel and expenses

Onsite days at a client's site in Bengaluru are billed at the day rate with no separate travel charge. For travel outside Bengaluru, we bill actual costs with prior written approval: economy air, 2AC or 3AC train, a hotel up to ₹8,000 a night in metro cities and ₹5,000 elsewhere, and a daily allowance of ₹2,500. A travel day is billed at 50% of the day rate. International travel is quoted separately. Receipts are kept for every claim.

## Onsite work in Bengaluru

Many Bengaluru clients sit in Whitefield, Manyata, Electronic City or along Outer Ring Road, where a journey at peak hours can take over an hour each way. Plan onsite days with a 10:30 IST start and agree the days in the proposal. Onsite days count as full day-rate days. We do not bill commute time. If a client asks for more than three onsite days a week, the proposal must say so and price it.

## Competitive pricing

We do not match a competitor's price. When a client raises a cheaper quote, ask what it includes, compare the scope line by line, and talk about delivery risk and the cost of rework. If the gap remains, bring the deal to the Head of Sales rather than lowering the price in the meeting. A price below the rate card is a discount and needs approval as set out in Approving larger discounts. Never criticise a competitor by name in writing.

## Competitive positioning

We meet three kinds of competitor. Large global integrators bring breadth and a big bench, but also layers of management and long timelines. Low-cost offshore suppliers are cheapest per day but rarely own the outcome. In-house teams and freelancers know the business but struggle to keep an AI system running after launch. Position on outcome ownership, speed to a working pilot and a named senior team. Do not name any real company in a proposal.

## Pricing a change request

A change request raised during an engagement is priced from the rate card in force on the date of the contract, for the roles involved, with no commitment discount unless the contract says so. The effort estimate comes from the delivery lead. The client sponsor approves the price in writing before work starts. GST is added as usual. Record every request, even one the account lead decides to absorb, together with the reason.

## Renewals

A renewal is priced from the rate card current on the renewal date. A retainer's rates may rise by at most 8% at renewal, and the client is told at least 60 days before. A client renewing for a longer term earns the matching commitment discount. A renewal is a new proposal and follows the same review thresholds. If a renewal is not signed by the end date, the Head of Sales decides whether work continues.
